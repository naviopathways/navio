Resources

Small tools for a more informed next step.

These original guides support career exploration and reflection. Use them independently, with a parent or guardian, or with an educator.

Explore programs ↗
✦
Resources are a supporting part of Navio.

They help students prepare and reflect, but they do not replace real experience, guarantee an outcome, or serve as a program application.

Tool 01EXPLORE / MAP

Career exploration

Research the work, not only the title.

Choose one career area and build a clearer picture before deciding whether it fits. Use more than one current source and separate what you know from what you still need to ask.

  1. Daily work: Which tasks fill a normal week, and which responsibilities matter most?
  2. Environment: Where, when, and with whom does the work usually happen?
  3. Pathways: Which education, training, experience, or portfolio routes can lead there?
  4. Tradeoffs: What pressures, constraints, or less-visible parts of the field should you understand?
  5. Test: What small project, conversation, course, or activity could help you explore further?
Tool 02EXPLORE / ASK

Professional conversation

Ask questions that reveal the real work.

A useful conversation goes beyond “How did you get your job?” Prepare questions that invite specific examples and help you understand what the field demands.

  • Surprise: What did you only understand after entering this field?
  • Skill: Which ability do beginners underestimate, and how can they practise it?
  • Reality: What does a normal week look like when the job is busy?
  • Change: How is technology or the market changing the work?
  • Next step: What is one low-risk way for a young person to test their interest?
Tool 03EXPERIENCE / REFLECT

Experience reflection

Turn an activity into evidence about yourself.

After a program, project, volunteer role, course, or event, capture what the experience actually showed. A title says very little; your choices and responses reveal far more.

SituationWhat was happening, and what made it challenging?

RoleWhat responsibility was yours rather than the team’s?

ActionWhich decision or contribution did you make?

ResultWhat changed, and what evidence supports that?

NextWhat would you repeat, change, or explore further?

Move from thinking to doing

Resources become more useful when an experience tests them.
